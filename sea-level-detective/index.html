How it avoids false alarms

Sea level remembers last month, so common tests cry “trend!” too often.

How El Niño moves the ocean

Trade winds weaken, warm water surges east: in 2015–16 the equatorial Pacific rose about 20 cm. In New York it arrives as more winter storm surges.

How we tested

Sea level in mm, 1993–2012 average = 0. Tide gauges measure against the land; satellites against the Earth's centre.

How we tested the clues

We counted storm-surge days in each October–April season, 1980–2025, and checked 29 clues from around the world. Each clue predicts a season with a model fitted on the other seasons, so no winter helps predict itself. Clues that looked useful on 1981–2009 then had to predict 2010–2025, winters they had never seen. El Niño, read in June, passed.

The sea-level detective

For the chosen place and years we fit a trend line plus the yearly cycle (and El Niño, when removed), and widen the uncertainty because sea level remembers last month. A rise counts as real when its 95% range stays above zero. “Years of records needed” follows Weatherhead et al. (1998).

Use of AI

AI tools (Claude, Codex, Kimi) helped with literature search, code and drafting. Every number comes from our open-source pipeline and was checked by the team.
