Case File / 01

Where is New York Harbor's winter written?

Could patterns in the tropical Pacific help us understand changes thousands of kilometres away on the coast?

Our team studies the relationship between large-scale ocean variability and local coastal water levels. We use satellite-based ocean observations alongside long-term measurements from coastal gauges.

Important distinction

The relationship between ENSO and monthly mean sea level has been explored in preliminary analyses. A specific claim about forecasting New York Harbor winter storm-surge days would require separate validation and is not established by that monthly analysis.

Working research question, not a verified flood forecast.

Investigator's Notebook / 04

How the models work

Two complementary approaches
Method A · Coast

Monthly time-series model

Separate a slowly changing baseline from seasonal patterns and possible contributions from ENSO, including time-lagged terms.

h(t) = a + bt + s(t) + Σ ckE(t − kΔt) + ε(t)

The seasonal term s(t) is represented by annual and semiannual sine/cosine harmonics; coefficients can be fitted by least squares.

Method B · Open Ocean

Spatial ocean dynamics

Investigate gridded satellite-derived ocean variables and surface winds to understand basin-scale patterns and possible physical mechanisms.

Observations → spatial analysis → model comparison

Physics-based modeling and validation belong here as separate work. Do not claim that a PINN or a specific current model has been validated unless confirmed by the team.
